About

I work in the Machine Learning group at Luleå University of Technology, Sweden, where I am supervised by Marcus Liwicki and Denis Kleyko (Örebro University). My research is on neuro-inspired architectures, specifically recurrent networks whose units are divided into excitatory and inhibitory populations. I study when such networks remain stable and whether that structure makes them more robust to corrupted input.

This work led to TIDE, written with both supervisors and accepted to NeurIPS 2026. TIDE extends the Continuous Thought Machine with Dale-constrained Wilson-Cowan dynamics, lateral inhibition, and a surprise-gated memory, and the paper supports the design with proofs of convergence and stability.

My earlier work was done in the university's Robotics and Artificial Intelligence group. There I worked on perception, which included filtering smoke out of LiDAR point clouds for search-and-rescue robots, segmenting power lines in LiDAR scans, and recording a multimodal dataset in an underground environment. I also worked on autonomous orbit management for satellites in low Earth orbit, using deep reinforcement learning.
